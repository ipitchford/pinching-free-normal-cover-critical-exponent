# Bounded novelty report

**Target:** AIM-TOPOLOGY-0203 / Kapovich's AIM Problem 4.3  
**Search updated:** 1 September 2026  
**Intended contribution unit:** theorem and complete proof  
**Outcome:** broad existential precedent located; no exact all-Fuchsian collision located

## Frozen statement

A fixed kernel of a closed surface group epimorphism onto `F_g` gives a family
of infinitely generated first-kind Fuchsian groups among the Fuchsian points
of one reduced, surface-based quasiconformal Teichmuller space. Along
simultaneous pinching of the cut curves, their critical exponents approach one
from below at the one-sided rate `1-delta = O_g(ell)`. Hence the exponent is
nonconstant and in fact takes infinitely many values.

Excluded: monotonicity, an exact exponent formula, classification, a
quasi-Fuchsian theorem, historical priority, peer-review status, and
injectivity of the finite-dimensional base family into `T_qc`.

## Construction fingerprint

1. Epimorphism `pi_1(S_g) -> F_g` killing a complete cut system.
2. Fixed normal subgroup and regular free-group cover.
3. Strict critical-exponent comparison from nonamenability.
4. Simultaneous collar degeneration on the compact base.
5. One-cell compactly supported Rayleigh test function.
6. Elstrodt--Patterson--Sullivan conversion to `delta -> 1`.

## Closest located sources

### Bishop (2003)

Bishop constructs infinitely generated groups and studies whether the critical
exponent equals the dimension of the limit set under quasi-Fuchsian
deformation. His tree-of-pieces construction also illustrates a first-kind
Fuchsian group with exponent below one. The inspected theorem does not provide
two Fuchsian critical exponents in one quasiconformal Teichmuller space, and
its non-delta-stability result concerns quasi-Fuchsian deformation in three
dimensions.

### Astala--Zinsmeister (1995)

Astala and Zinsmeister use a compact genus-three surface and an abelian normal
cover with deck group `Z^3`, then vary its Poincare exponent through
holomorphic quasi-Fuchsian deformations. This is the closest located exponent-
variation precedent. It does not duplicate the present all-Fuchsian family:
their deformation leaves the Fuchsian locus and produces values above one,
whereas the present nonamenable free cover stays Fuchsian, has exponent below
one for every marked compact-base metric, and approaches one from below. Under
the broad group-deformation reading, their work appears already to answer the
existential AIM question; the present novelty unit is the all-Fuchsian
strengthening and fixed-kernel quantitative construction.

### Bonfert-Taylor--Matsuzaki--Taylor (2012)

Their regular-cover results show that the lower threshold `1/2` for exponents
of regular covers of closed hyperbolic surfaces is sharp but not attained.
They do not state the fixed-kernel pinching deformation used here.

### Huo--Zinsmeister (2022)

Their Ruelle-property results extend part of the Astala--Zinsmeister line and
study exponent and limit-set-dimension behaviour under quasi-Fuchsian
deformation. They do not give the present all-Fuchsian reduced-space family.

### Lehnert (2017)

Lehnert proves that certain infinitely generated first-kind Veech groups have
critical exponent strictly between `1/2` and `1`, using nonamenability of a
Schreier graph. This confirms the exponent regime but does not locate
variation inside a fixed `T_qc`.

### Dougall--Sharp (2016)

Their theorem supplies the strict compact-base inequality for the present
normal cover. It does not vary the ambient compact metric or state the
pinching consequence.

### Sullivan (1987), Keen (1974), and standard degeneration theory

These sources supply the spectral conversion and collars. The bounded search
did not locate the present bridge—fixed free normal cover plus simultaneous
pinching—as a stated solution of the AIM question.

### Recent and broader searches

Searches also surfaced work on random finite covers, spectral tightness of
normal covers, critical exponents for surfaces with long boundary, and
critical exponents of graph/Teichmuller representations. These have different
groups, deformation spaces, or asymptotic parameters and did not match the
frozen statement.
